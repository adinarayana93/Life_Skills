# Listening and Active Communication

## Question 1
### What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?

- Focus on the speaker instead of getting distracted by my own thoughts.
- Do not interrupt; let the person finish speaking.
- Use simple phrases like “I understand” or “Tell me more” to keep the conversation going.
- Show that I am listening through eye contact, nodding, and body language.
- Take notes when the conversation has important details.
- Paraphrase what the person said to make sure I understood it correctly.

## Question 2
### According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?

- Focus fully on the conversation and remove distractions.
- Try to understand the speaker's point of view without judging them.
- Notice their mood, tone, and body language.
- Summarize what they said in a clear way.
- Respond to the point they are actually discussing.
- Give enough silence instead of filling every gap with words.
- Repeat the process when the topic changes and make sure both people understand each other.

## Question 3
### What are the obstacles in your listening process?

- I sometimes think about my reply while the other person is still speaking.
- Distractions from my phone or surroundings can reduce my attention.
- Sometimes I assume I already understand the point and stop listening carefully.
- Technical discussions can have many small details that are easy to miss.
- I may focus more on the solution than on fully understanding the problem.

## Question 4
### What can you do to improve your listening?

- Keep my phone away during important conversations.
- Let the speaker finish before responding.
- Take notes during meetings and technical discussions.
- Ask questions when something is unclear.
- Repeat or summarize important points to confirm my understanding.
- Focus on the speaker's words, tone, and body language.

## Question 5
### When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?

- When I am not confident about my opinion.
- When I want to avoid an argument.
- When I feel uncomfortable saying “no”.
- Sometimes when speaking to someone senior, I may hesitate to express my point clearly.

## Question 6
### When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

- When I am frustrated and feel that someone is not listening.
- When I strongly disagree with someone and react too quickly.
- When I am under pressure and focus more on proving my point than understanding theirs.

## Question 7
### When do you switch to Passive-Aggressive communication styles in your day-to-day life?

- When I am annoyed but do not say the actual problem directly.
- Sometimes I use sarcasm instead of clearly explaining what bothered me.
- I may become quiet or avoid a conversation when I am upset.
- I may expect the other person to understand my feelings without telling them directly.

## Question 8
### How can you make your communication assertive?

- Say what I need clearly and directly.
- Speak respectfully without blaming the other person.
- Use “I” statements instead of accusations.
- Listen to the other person's point before responding.
- Say “no” when necessary without being rude.
- Ask clear questions when something is unclear.
- Stay calm even when I disagree.
- Focus on solving the problem instead of trying to win the argument.
